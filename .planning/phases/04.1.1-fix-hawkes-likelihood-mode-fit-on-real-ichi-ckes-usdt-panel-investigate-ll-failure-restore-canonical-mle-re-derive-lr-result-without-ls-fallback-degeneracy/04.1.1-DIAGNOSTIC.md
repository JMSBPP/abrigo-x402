## 1. HEADLINE VERDICT — **YES (CONDITIONAL): η is trustworthy; signal is REAL, magnitude is biased LOW.**

The canonical scipy MLE wrapping `_hawkes_loglik_vectorized` is **sound**. When fed data that
tick *genuinely* simulated at η=0.5 with a decay matched to the event spacing, the estimator
recovers η̂≈0.54 (free-β, n≈700) and η̂≈0.43 (fixed-β-at-truth, n≈700) — within tolerance, and
biased *toward zero*. The real-panel AIC-selected fit (β=0.001, η=0.628) is a stable interior
trough at a physically sensible timescale (1/β=1000s ≈ the 665s pooled / ~1300s per-leg
inter-arrival). Because the only estimator bias runs *downward*, the true real-panel η is **at
least** ~0.6 and plausibly higher — this strengthens, never weakens, an η≥0.2 self-excitation
claim.

**Deciding evidence (the discovery that flips the prior conclusion):** the original
"synthetic η=0.5 fixture recovers only η≈0.05" was **NOT** a β-spacing mismatch. It is a
**kernel-normalization labeling error in the fixture**. `tick.SimuHawkesExpKernels` uses the
*normalized* kernel φ(t)=α·β·e^(−βt), so tick's branching ratio is ρ(α), **not** ρ(α/β). The
committed fixture (`A=0.025, β=0.1`) is labeled `expected_branching_ratio=0.5` but
`tick.spectral_radius()` reports **0.05** — it was always a near-Poisson η=0.05 panel. The MLE
"failing" to find η=0.5 was the MLE *correctly* recovering the η=0.05 that was actually
simulated. There is no estimator defect. (See §2/Q1 table.)

**Residual uncertainty:** finite-sample downward bias at n≈700 (~14%, η̂≈0.43 vs 0.5); the
real-panel η(β) curve is monotone-decreasing in β so the headline number is decay-conditional —
but β is AIC-pinned at an interior, well-separated minimum (ΔAIC≈22 vs nearest neighbor), so
the conditioning is data-driven, not a free knob.

## 3. THE [0.283, 0.371] BAND IS A CONSTRAINED-PROJECTION ARTIFACT — **CONFIRMED**

`profile_likelihood.py` does **not** invert a joint MLE. Mechanics (lines 72–118):

1. For each grid η it calls `fit_hawkes_with_fixed_branching_ratio` (hawkes_fit.py L269), which
   fits **unconstrained**, then **rescales the adjacency by `eta_target/eta_current`** (the
   "projection trick", L298–300) — it does NOT re-optimise the LL subject to ρ(α/β)=η.
2. Critically (L114): `LL_max := max_k profile_LL(grid)` — the reference is the **max over the
   projected family**, NOT the true MLE log-likelihood. The reported `eta_hat` is the **grid
   argmax** (L118), and `eta_hat_unconstrained` in the artifact is **0.000307** — the degenerate
   LS-fallback η, completely disconnected from the 0.341 reported `eta_hat`.
3. The fit_report records `fit_method_used="least-squares"`, η_unconstrained=0.0003, decays=0.1.
   So the [0.283, 0.371] CI was derived by: take a **degenerate LS fit at the kernel-blind β=0.1**,
   project its (near-zero) adjacency up to each grid η, and invert a χ²(1) deficit around the
   grid argmax of that *projected* surface.

This band is therefore an artifact of (a) the LS-fallback degeneracy, (b) the kernel-blind β=0.1,
and (c) the projection trick standing in for a genuine constrained MLE. It is **not** a
confidence interval for the joint-MLE η. The honest joint-MLE η at the AIC-selected β=0.001 is
0.628 (Q2) — far outside [0.283, 0.371]. The band must be discarded and re-derived from the
corrected fit.
